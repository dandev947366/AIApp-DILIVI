HAMK UNIVERSITY THESIS AND AI GUIDELINES 2026

--- SECTION 1: THE THESIS PROCESS CHECKLIST ---
The thesis process contains at least the following stages. 
Note: SC = Send for External Comment.

1. Start of the thesis process:
- Topic template retrieved from Moodle.
- Supervisor name added to the topic form after the topic seminar.
- Topic form copied to Wihi.

2. Planning stage:
- Plan template retrieved from Moodle.
- Plan uploaded to Wihi after plan seminar.
- A signed thesis contract uploaded to Wihi, if you have a client. Supervisor takes the file from Wihi and signs.
- Possible research permits acquired and stored to Wihi.

3. Implementation stage:
- Thesis template retrieved from Moodle.
- Content management plan written to thesis annex.
- Zotero installed and taken into use.
- After 5-10 pages writing sent to language teacher with Wihi Post for comment.

4. Interim seminar:
- Thesis sent to opponent around one week before the interim seminar.
- Opposing report template retrieved from Moodle.
- Opposing report completed and sent to the supervisor of the seminar over Wihi SC.
- (SC to this supervisor, if not own supervisor).
- Opposing report completed and sent to the opposed writer.

5. Final Seminar:
- 20 min final seminar ppt stored to Wihi.
- (SC to this supervisor, if not own supervisor).
- Maturity exam done and stored to Wihi.

6. Final stages (not necessarily in this order):
- Thesis stored to Wihi for final evaluation.
- SC to language teacher for language check.
- SC to client.
- Plagiarism check to Ouriginal done in Wihi.
- Final version stored in Wihi and Theseus, pdf/a format.

--- SECTION 2: AI USE GUIDELINES ---
Students must read the AI-use guidelines before beginning their thesis. AI must be clearly disclosed.

Acceptable applications of AI:
- Topic idea brainstorming.
- Consultation on research questions (identifying gaps).
- Finding appropriate research methods.
- Benchmarking state-of-the-art research.
- Exploring literature and summarizing.
- Support writing process (enhancing readability, but must review and edit).
- Coding assistance (explanations of logic; must understand and validate all code).
- Visualization (producing infographics).
- Using AI as a simulated client to test ideas.

Unacceptable applications of AI:
- Do not share any sensitive or confidential data.
- AI tools (such as ChatGPT, Copilot, or Claude) must NOT be used to generate the substantive content of the thesis.
- Do not trust results blindly; verify outputs.
- Use of AI during the maturity exam is strictly forbidden.
- Submitting AI-generated work as your own is plagiarism/fraud.

AI Disclosure Requirements:
Disclose AI in the "Methodology" section or at the end. Include:
1. Name of AI tool(s).
2. Description of task.
3. Specific prompt(s) used (optional).
4. How the output was used.

--- SECTION 3: ETHICAL AND LEGAL RECOMMENDATIONS ---
- Conflicts of Interest: Must ascertain possible conflicts of interest.
- Research Ethics: Follow good research practices (TENK RI 2023).
- Data Protection (GDPR): Only process necessary data. Special categories (health, ethnicity) require explicit consent.
- Public Document: Theses are public documents (Act on the Openness of Government Activities) published in Theseus.

--- SECTION 4: HAMK REFERENCING GUIDE (APA 7) ---
HAMK uses APA 7 style. Plagiarism includes copying text without references.

Citing AI as a Source (Specific Chat):
In-text: (OpenAI, 2025)
Reference: OpenAI. (2025, Aug 23). Title of chat in italics [Generative AI chat]. ChatGPT. URL

Citing AI as Software:
In-text: (OpenAI, 2025)
Reference: OpenAI. (2025). ChatGPT [Large language model]. https://chatgpt.com/

Citing AI-Generated Images:
Figure caption must state it was generated with AI.
Reference: OpenAI. (2024). DALL·E [Image generation model]. https://openai.com/dall-e

--- SECTION 5: THESIS STRUCTURE, THEORY, AND METHODS ---
A standard 30-page Bachelor's thesis (graded 3) usually follows this formula:
- Theory (Technology + Target Environment) + Methods (Agile, Prototype, etc.) + Practical Part (PoC, MVP, Test, Plan) + Results.

Theoretical Part:
- Written in your own words based on sources.
- Must describe both the technology (e.g., Flutter, React, UiPath) and the target environment (e.g., game world, banking, service desk).

Methods and Data Collection:
- How do you get new information? (Development project, diary, interviews, surveys, SWOT, action research, case study).
- Data collection methods: Diary, screenshots, files, Webropol.
- Requires a Data Management Plan (appendix 1).

Practical Part:
- Can range from a pure literature review to a requirements specification, prototype, product, or comparison.
- Results: What are the benefits of your thesis? What data supports your claim?

Introduction and Summary:
- Both should be about one page (like mirrors).
- Introduction: Leads the reader in, presents research questions.
- Summary: Short text on research question success, own learning, and future ideas.

--- SECTION 6: WIHI SYSTEM INSTRUCTIONS ---
Wihi is the thesis management system.

1. Logging in:
- Go to pakki.hamk.fi and click the Wihi icon, or go directly to wihi.hamk.fi. Log in with HAMK ID.

2. Topic Proposal:
- Select "Create a topic proposal".
- Fill mandatory fields (Thesis title in English is critical and transfers to your degree certificate).
- Add commissioning information if applicable.

3. Thesis Plan and Schedule:
- Once approved, upload the preliminary thesis plan (PDF), schedule, and thesis agreement (if commissioning).
- Supervisor will approve it.

4. External Comments:
- You can send your thesis to the commissioner using "Send for external comments". The link is valid for 21 days.

5. Phases:
- Phase 1: Planning phase.
- Phase 2: Implementation phase (upload thesis as PDF).

6. Plagiarism Check (Phase 3):
- Upload the final PDF (max 20 MB).

- Wait for analysis. The supervisor accepts or rejects it.

7. Publishing:
- Once evaluated, add the URN link to your published thesis in Theseus to Wihi.

--- SECTION 7: RESEARCH QUESTION EXAMPLES ---
A good thesis typically has around three research questions (e.g., 1 for Theory, 1 for Methods, 1 for Outcome).

Examples:
- What are the criteria for developing application S?
- What process should application S follow?
- What technologies should be used to develop Application S?
- At what cost can service P be implemented in different cloud services?
- How can costs be optimized for service P in different cloud services?
- What system can be used to analyze Company Y's sales data into the most flexible dashboard?
- What are the best practices when Company Y does Case A?
